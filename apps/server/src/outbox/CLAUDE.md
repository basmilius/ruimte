# The outbox, tasks and lineage

When a task settles, what wakes a chat and what runs on a clock are product rules in the root `CLAUDE.md`.

- The daemon, not a client, starts the agents a verb creates, through a durable entry in `$RUIMTE_HOME/outbox` that survives a restart. The store and worker are `@adecore/agents/outbox`. Ruimte's entry kinds and their contents are in `outbox/outbox.ts`, handlers beside it; task handlers are in `@adecore/agents/tasks` (bound in `tasks/wiring.ts`), message handlers in `@adecore/agents/messages`. Lineage (`AgentLineageStore`) and the mode ceiling are `@adecore/agents/lineage.ts` and `modes.ts`. A running turn is resumed from the outbox too (`resume-run.ts`). Session and chat managers create one id at a time.
- Chat observers settle tasks (`@adecore/agents/tasks/task-coordinator.ts`), never a clock. Entries due later:
    - `resume-limit` (`@adecore/agents/chat/limit-resume.ts`), `background-limit` (`@adecore/agents/tasks/background-limit.ts`) and `deliver-waiting` (`@adecore/agents/tasks/waiting-child.ts`).
    - `connect-peer` (`remote/connect-peer.ts`), the reconnect to another machine, at most one per machine under the target `peer:<machineId>`. Each failure writes a fresh entry with the wait doubled from one minute up to ten; a restart makes it due at once. It also gives up at once when that machine refuses this one's key or statement (another Ruimte version is waited out instead). Giving up fails the tasks still owed there (`RemoteTasks.giveUp`). An agent that ran there, or whose start may have reached it, is owed a stop, asked once over a link that happens to be open, never opening one (`once`). New work starts a fresh window.
    - `routine-run`, due at a routine's next time, written only by `routines/routines.ts`, at most one per routine under the target `routine:<projectId>:<routineId>`. It outlives its target, its handler rereads the routine from disk, and one due in the past after a restart runs once as a caught-up run.
- Entries due at once: `deliver-pr-watch` (`outbox/outbox.ts`, handled in `wiring.ts`), written only by the PR-watch (`github/pr-watch.ts`), which owns the clock and the 7 days; it opens one turn in the watching chat, which wakes nobody. `run-after-settle`, written only when a worktree chat settles (`launches/after-settle.ts`); it starts the project's chosen task there, never twice per turn, and only notes in the chat.
- The outbox timer never waits more than a minute (`wakeful-clock.ts`), so a time that passed during sleep is seen within a minute of waking. Work due later holds no lane, so nothing owed after it waits for it.
- While a `resume-limit` is owed, nothing else the outbox owes that chat opens a turn (`@adecore/agents/chat/wake-chat.ts`); a turn before the reset would hit the same limit and spend a try.
- The tasks of one `team --task` call (`batchId`) wake the parent once, when all settled. A wake that finds its chat in a turn answers `wait` and runs again when `OutboxWorker.wake` names that chat.
- After a restart a `background-limit` entry becomes due at once (`restarted`), since its commands went down with the CLI.
- Stopping or deleting a node ends its descendants through an `end-children` entry (`@adecore/agents/tasks/end-children.ts`, bound to Ruimte's chats, terminals and kinds in `end-children.ts`), written before the node goes so a restart still ends them; their threads and screens stay. Two nodes whose opener no project places are no orphans when the project is indexed again: one another machine's agent opened (`peer` on its record), and a chat a Voice session started (`openedBy` = `voice:<sessionId>`), which outlives that session.
- No observer here settles a task whose child runs on another machine (`machine` on the task). That machine reports it, and `settleFromElsewhere` settles it the usual way, waking the parent once. `connect-peer` counts four entry kinds as owed to the machine:
    - `start-remote-agent` (target the node): waits for the link and a place on the machine; neither ever fails the task.
    - `take-remote-result` (target the node): waits for the machine's word, takes in the result and branch, and acknowledges.
    - `stop-remote-agent` (target `peer:<machineId>`): outlives the node.
    - `deliver-remote-result` (target `peer:<machineId>`), on the machine doing the work: stays until the other machine acknowledges.
- An ended node drops its `start-remote-agent` and owes a stop only for an agent that started (`remote/remote-tasks.ts`).
